# Foundation content and design guardrails

Use this file only when a task changes public copy, information architecture, design, metadata, structured data, imagery, or factual claims.

## Current direction

The September 17, 2026 editorial and conversion system is the approved parent-site direction and supersedes earlier homepage, navigation, and campaign language.

- Keep the primary navigation compact: What we do, Research, About, Contact. Keep the canonical `/platforms` route unless a separate migration is approved.
- Keep the homepage opening exactly: “Evidence should lead somewhere.” Do not replace, rotate, or reinterpret it without an explicit user instruction.
- The parent story is evidence moving into practical use across health access, cybersecurity experiential learning, public-interest research, and responsible AI.
- Keep the parent homepage broader than publications. Use a natural path from premise to evidence to current work to proof to engagement.
- Keep Health, Place Intelligence, CB-CAP, AI Lab, and other subdomains out of scope unless the user explicitly includes them.

## Visual and verbal system

- No visible eyebrow labels, repetitive label/headline/body/CTA templates, boxed card grids, generic nonprofit imagery, generated hero art, decorative CSS art, fake dashboards, or website screenshots used as campaign imagery.
- Prefer typography, whitespace, asymmetry, verified evidence, real program photography, publication covers, leadership portraits, Health photography, AI Lab project evidence, and verified CB-CAP data.
- Use the approved cobalt, deep navy, royal blue, icy blue, white, and supporting gray palette. No green as the parent-site color and no gradients.
- Keep the existing geometric sans-serif voice; serif typography is not the primary brand voice.
- Use MGI for research hierarchy, Brookings for editorial provenance, Gates Foundation for parent-brand storytelling, and P&G for purposeful campaign storytelling. Deloitte, Accenture, and McKinsey are quality references only. Never copy layouts/language or imply affiliation.
- Keep copy concise, specific, human, and problem/evidence/response/action led. Avoid generic consulting language, AI-sounding prose, internal planning language, and mechanical process writing.
- Do not expose prompts, TODOs, release labels, technical debt, maintenance notes, implementation plans, or other internal workflow language in public surfaces or metadata.

## Evidence and claim boundaries

- Never invent endorsements, partners, funding, events, impact, adoption, validation, certification, or outcomes.
- Participant-reported figures must remain visibly attributed and limited. Do not restate them as independent estimates or achieved outcomes.
- The direct primary-care access pilot is planned for 2027. The first cohort is 25 people to test the nonclinical pathway, identify failure points, and improve the model before expansion.
- The health-access story may use the verified 2025 Western New York preplanning roundtable and REBS evidence. “More than 12,000 residents per primary care clinician” must remain participant-reported context. Do not publish wait-time, travel-duration, or similar figures until the exact source is verified.
- Present cybersecurity experiential learning as a recurring Capella University collaboration in which graduate cybersecurity learners move from instruction into six months of applied work. Capability language may include IAM, GRC, cloud security, risk, least privilege, and evidence assurance. Capella retains academic ownership, oversight, and grading. Do not imply endorsement.
- Do not describe Health Systems Assurance as validated, certified, proven, adopted, or a standard. Present its constructs as proposed analytical tools and a research agenda.
- AI and Society should explain the public output, participation pathway, institutional response pathway, and what support enables. Do not name prospective funders, technology providers, or partners unless verified and approved for publication.
- Describe roundtables, participation, partnerships, funding, cataloging, and library holdings precisely. Participation or cataloging is not endorsement, adoption, or a continuing institutional commitment.

## Pages, publications, leadership, and legal

- Preserve `/publication/hsa-v1-2026`, `/publication/rrg-v1-2025`, and `/publication/rebs-v1-2025` as individual publication routes.
- Research pages should state why the work exists, who can use it, what decision it can inform, evidence limits, and a clear access/engagement path.
- Keep Home, What we do, Research, Insights, Events, About, Partner, Support, Standards, platform overviews, and publication landing pages as real pages.
- Include the existing leadership team with approved portraits, titles, and profiles. Do not call Dr. Oluwabiyi Adeyemo “founder.” Keep the Foundation-specific title currently approved in the repository unless explicitly changed by the user.
- Keep rural health and rural equity visible, but do not define the Foundation as rural-only. Parent positioning also covers health access, cybersecurity experiential learning, public-interest research, governance, community evidence, participation, and responsible AI.
- The footer must identify The SozoRock Foundation, Inc. as a 501(c)(3) public charity and include the verified EIN. Legal links remain concise: Privacy, Terms, Accessibility, Nondiscrimination, Standards. Keep Partner and Support separate from legal links.
- Legal/policy pages must use direct institutional language, preserve platform boundaries, and avoid promises of certification, absolute security, uninterrupted service, or support that has not been arranged.
